## Suggested settings for this data

### 1. Final LR is essentially zero — scheduler decayed too aggressively

**Current → Suggested**
- `final_lr`: 3.05e-9 → target ≥ 1e-5 at end of run

**Why**: A final LR of 3.05e-9 is below what any practical optimizer can act on. Even if training started at 1e-3, a decay to 3e-9 means the LR dropped by a factor of ~328,000. In later epochs, weight updates were effectively zero — the model was burning compute without learning. This almost certainly means:
- If `scheduler: step` was used: the step size fired too frequently or gamma was too aggressive (e.g. gamma=0.1 every few epochs)
- If ExponentialLR was actually used (see next point): the per-epoch gamma was too small (e.g. gamma=0.9 for 100+ epochs)

**Action**: Re-train with a scheduler that reaches no lower than 1e-5 at the end of the epoch budget. For ExponentialLR from 1e-3 over 100 epochs targeting 1e-5: gamma ≈ 0.907 (`(1e-5 / 1e-3) ** (1/100)`). For StepLR: step_size=20, gamma=0.5, max 4 steps → final 1e-3 × 0.0625 = 6.25e-5.

---

### 2. Scheduler name conflicts with model directory name

**Current**: `scheduler: "step"` in meta.json, model directory named `TSLA5Min_LR_Exponential_Decay`

**Why**: The model name implies ExponentialLR was intended, but meta.json records `step`. Either:
- The wrong scheduler was selected when training was started
- The model was renamed after training without updating the config

**Action**: Decide which scheduler is intended before the next run. ExponentialLR with a well-tuned gamma is simpler to reason about than StepLR for this use case — one parameter (gamma per epoch) vs two (step_size + gamma).

---

### 3. vol_return has an extreme outlier that may cause gradient spikes

**Current**: `vol_return` range = -0.999 to **1836.1** (mean=3.4, std=44.7)  
**Pre-scale spread ratio**: 40,358× (vol_return widest; rolling_vol narrowest)

**Why**: A vol_return of 1836 means one bar had 1836× the rolling average volume. RobustScaler uses median + IQR so its *scaling parameters* are unaffected by this, but after scaling, this bar will produce a value hundreds of standard deviations above the median. Any window containing it will produce outsized gradients even with Adam, and the autoencoder will be asked to reconstruct an extreme outlier it can't handle gracefully.

Note: Alpaca free-tier (IEX) data has partial volume reporting — this spike may be a data artifact rather than a real event. `trade_count_ratio` max of 16.06 is less severe but worth watching for the same reason.

**Action**: Clip `vol_return` during `add_features()` before the scaler sees it:
```python
df['vol_return'] = df['vol_return'].clip(upper=df['vol_return'].quantile(0.99))
```
The 99th percentile of vol_return is likely ~10–20× (consistent with real volume spikes), which is representable and learnable. This is a one-line addition to `add_features()` in `storage.py`.
